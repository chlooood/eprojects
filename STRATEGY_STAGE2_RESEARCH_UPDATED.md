# TerraCorridor — Strategy Stage 2 (Research-Updated)

**Updated November 21, 2026 after reviewing `05_Research_Report (pipelines and risks, 150 sources).md`.** This update preserves the original Stage 2 plan and records the changes in evidence and recommended priorities. The report states that it draws on 150 distinct sources; some were only partially read. Claims below attributed to the report are not independently reverified.

## Research update — what changes our decision

**Revised recommendation:** Retain *predictive right-of-way intelligence* as the company vision. Make **permit-based development early warning connected to preliminary corridor work review** the initial product *experiment*. Keep integrity-dig readiness as the nearest recurring-work expansion, not a proven beachhead. Test operators and engineering consultants equally. The recommendation may change after practitioner interviews and an independent forecast backtest.

**Why the order changes:** The report provides strong evidence that damage prevention, geohazard analysis, class monitoring and land obligations are paid-for activities, but also finds substantial incumbent coverage. Irth combines much of the workflow; Pandell serves land and crossing users; Cambio has a very large geohazard footprint; Stantec and GDM already address development/class-location monitoring. We cannot win solely because three kinds of information appear in one interface. The plausible opening is *earlier actionable source information* flowing into a review that existing systems do not prepare as effectively. This remains a hypothesis.

### Revised validation priorities before KickStart

**Priority 1 — Interview the current workflow, not the idea.** Ask at least three operator-side and three consultant-side practitioners how development permits, municipal referrals, imagery, crossing requests and land constraints reach them. Ask for a recent dated example. Specifically identify what arrives before and after the formal review begins.

**Priority 2 — Measure early warning.** On Calgary or another legitimately usable, labelled corridor, reconstruct what was knowable at an earlier historical date. Compare first permit signal, baseline imagery/building evidence, operator referral date where available, and confirmed change. Report median lead, misses, false alerts and data gaps. If verified Canadian class labels cannot be established, measure the narrower claim of *earlier development detection*, not regulatory classification accuracy.

**Priority 3 — Show a preliminary case packet.** Use the existing n8n watch and human review layers to generate an evidence-backed candidate review with hazard context and a clearly illustrative land obligation. State `unverified` when required information is missing. Do not label it work-authorized or compliance-approved.

**Priority 4 — Test the recurring paid workflow.** With a consultant or operator, time preparation of five to ten appropriately shared historical crossing/integrity-dig cases, compare quality as well as speed, and identify what incumbent software already automates. Work readiness leads commercially only if this shows genuine additional value.

**Priority 5 — Resolve the actual data and buyer gates.** Verify AER licence rights, corridor route quality, access to real agreements, security constraints, and whether an economic buyer authorizes a pilot.

### Conditional market decision

- **If historical permits give meaningful, reliable lead time that current workflows do not capture:** lead with *development early warning integrated into corridor review*; offer it as a feed or lightweight review workspace, including through consultants.
- **If permit lead time adds little, but existing work packets still require costly manual preparation:** lead with *work readiness*, using geohazard and land obligations as verified inputs; this demands stronger private-data permissions.
- **If established tools already cover both and no buyer identifies measurable gaps:** do not broaden the platform to manufacture a market; pause the commercial claim and validate another problem.

**What does not change:** The company mission remains right-of-way resilience, the MVP must keep human approvals and honest data labels, and operators and consultants are tested equally. Market-size scenarios later in this document are still assumptions and should not be treated as verified demand. The original section recommending integrity-dig readiness as the lead demo should now be read as the **secondary demo/workflow experiment**, not the sole beachhead conclusion.

# TerraCorridor — Strategy Stage 2

**Decision and execution plan | November 21, 2026 | Team of four**  
**Audience:** TerraCorridor team, KickStart mentors, and potential pilot partners  
**Status:** Working strategy. Market, technical, and customer claims are separated below.

## Executive decision

**Recommendation:** Develop TerraCorridor as **right-of-way (ROW) change and work-readiness intelligence** for pipeline infrastructure. Begin by testing **two routes to market equally**: pipeline operators and engineering/land/environmental consultants. Compare three paid workflows: (1) early development screening, (2) crossing and encroachment review preparation, and (3) integrity-dig and maintenance work readiness. **Lead the demo with work readiness** because it naturally uses development, geohazards, and land obligations together. **Keep development monitoring as the strongest technical difference**, not the whole business.

**Customer promise:** “Know what is changing along a pipeline corridor, what a planned activity needs, and what must be reviewed before work starts.”

**Proposed positioning:** *TerraCorridor — From right-of-way change to work-ready decisions.*

This is a recommendation to test, **not** evidence that customers will buy it. No customer interview, paid pilot, or verified saving is established in the inspected materials. A narrower class-location product remains the fallback if it wins customer validation.

### The decision in plain language

Pipeline owners must monitor their corridors and coordinate work near them. A new subdivision, changing ground conditions, or a landowner notice requirement can affect the work they plan. Those facts are often checked through several processes. TerraCorridor aims to assemble the facts, flag missing information, and route a proposed action to the right person. It **does not** authorize digging, certify pipeline safety, replace an engineer, or grant land access.

## 2. Which recurring paid workflow deserves the product?

| Workflow | Evidence of demand | Why it fits | Competitive or practical weakness | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Early development screening** | Operators have class-location monitoring responsibilities; Stantec documents an ATCO Class Location Watch project. [S1], [S5] | Existing Edmonton permit forecasting; possible notice before construction | Incumbents also forecast development; alert frequency and incremental lead time unproven | **Differentiating input; test as add-on** |
| **B. Crossing / encroachment review preparation** | CER consent requests have a 10-working-day response rule in relevant cases; Irth reports Enbridge cutting routine reviews from about 60 to 5 minutes and $100,000+ annual savings. [S2], [S3] | Repeated requests, structured checks, land conditions, audit records | Irth already does this well; replacing incumbent review software is a weak entry strategy | **Strong demand, difficult direct competition** |
| **C. Integrity-dig / maintenance work readiness** | Vertex reports continued paid land and regulatory coordination for an annual integrity-dig program since 2018. [S4] | Geohazards + land obligations + site conditions directly influence work preparation; easy to demonstrate one case | Real agreements and work packages may be confidential; other consultants/software already serve it | **Best integrated MVP and pilot hypothesis** |

**Decision:** Test C as the headline workflow, B as the measurable economic benchmark, and A as the predictive data layer. Do **not** claim the market is unserved. The defensible question is whether TerraCorridor **fills a gap before or between existing systems**.

**Important legal distinction:** CER rules differ by activity and operator jurisdiction. A 10-working-day response obligation concerns certain requests for written consent, not every review or every pipeline. Under CER guidance, certain work within a 30 m prescribed area requires authorization; this is not a universal ban on all work. Class-location filing obligations apply under specific conditions and must not be treated as an automatic countdown for every new building. [S2], [S6], [S7]

## 3. Operator versus consultant: equal market test

| Question | Pipeline operator | Engineering / ROW consultant |
|---|---|---|
| Daily user | Integrity engineer, land/ROW coordinator, crossings analyst | Project manager, land agent, GIS analyst, environmental reviewer |
| Economic buyer to identify | Integrity/operations/land department head | Practice leader, operations director, digital-delivery lead |
| Existing spend | Staff, consultants, GIS, integrity and work systems | Staff hours, reporting tools, software licenses, project delivery |
| Why pay | Reduce review preparation, missed constraints and planning delays | Complete more projects with consistent quality and less rework |
| Best pilot | One corridor and 5–10 historical or anonymized work cases | 5–10 old cases from different client projects, with permission |
| Main obstacle | Security review, incumbent integrations, engineering assurance | Tool may reduce billable hours; reuse of client data may be restricted |
| Route to scaling | Expand to more lines, teams and work types within one operator | License across projects; potential distribution to client operators |

**Neutral recommendation:** Approach **five operator-side professionals and five consultant-side professionals** with the same core interview questions. Choose the first paying segment from evidence, not preference. A consultant offering a real test set could be a faster entry route; an operator identifying a costly internal bottleneck could be the better long-term buyer.

**Decision rules by November 27:**

- Prefer a segment that provides **at least one real, appropriately authorized sample workflow**, a named internal sponsor, and a clear measurable pain point.
- If either segment agrees to a defined pilot (even non-binding) and the other does not, prioritize the one with practical access.
- If both are positive, choose based on repeat use, contract ownership, data permission and opportunity to expand within the account.
- If neither confirms a meaningful problem, reduce the product to **class-location early warning** and investigate whether it is worth paying for; do not invent validation.

## 5. Business model and pricing to test

**First offering:** paid or sponsored pilot for a **single corridor or defined project batch**, with human review and explicitly limited scope. **Longer-term:** annual subscription by operator portfolio or consultant practice, potentially with usage bands for corridors / cases. Do not charge individual landowners or residents.

| Possible buyer | Pilot offer | Expansion offer | Pricing status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Operator | Review preparation on 5–10 historic cases, compare time and accuracy with current workflow | Annual license for selected lines and teams; integration and onboarding separately | **Hypothesis only** |
| Consultant | Limited practice trial with approved anonymized cases | Practice license or per-project use, with client-data segregation | **Hypothesis only** |

**Value formula to validate:**

`annual net value = (cases/year × verified minutes saved/case ÷ 60 × loaded hourly labour cost) + separately proven avoided rework costs − software − onboarding − verification − integration costs`

Avoid counting theoretical “avoided pipeline failures,” “prevented replacement” or regulatory penalties as product savings without causal evidence. Record review **quality** alongside speed: a faster but incomplete packet is not an improvement.

## 6. TAM, SAM and SOM — honest bottom-up assumptions (CAD)

These figures are **planning scenarios, not a published market-revenue estimate**. Market overlaps and eligibility matter. AER reports **955 companies** operating AER-regulated pipelines in 2025, but **77 operated over 80% of the inventory**; most are not automatically suitable enterprise customers. PHMSA's 2025 gas transmission and gathering annual-report dataset contains **1,445 submitted reports**, not 1,445 unique buyers of this product. Do not add those totals or treat every report as a separate purchaser. [S9], [S10]

**Definition:** Qualified accounts are organizations with meaningful corridor work volume, budget, data rights and a workflow not already adequately served. A consultant and its operator client must not be double-counted if both are included in one channel forecast.

| Market layer | Illustrative qualified accounts | Assumed annual contract | Annual opportunity | What is unverified |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| **TAM:** North American eligible operator + consultant accounts, after deduplication | 300–1,000 | $20k–$100k | **$6m–$100m** | Exact qualifying account count and willingness to pay |
| **SAM:** Initial Western Canada / adjacent North American pipeline workflow segment | 75–250 | $25k–$75k | **$1.875m–$18.75m** | Geographic reach, project volume and buyer budget |
| **SOM:** Year-three commercial objective | 5–15 paying accounts | $30k–$60k | **$150k–$900k ARR** | Procurement cycle, pilot conversion, delivery ability |

These are **ranges across scenarios**, not confidence intervals. Avoid presenting the upper endpoint alone as a validated market. A defensible pitch slide can say: *“We are testing a focused North American software opportunity; buyer counts and pricing are being validated with operators and engineering consultants.”*

**Expansion decision:** Other linear assets (rail, power transmission, water corridors) are a **possible second TAM**, not current SAM. Their geology, data, legal duties and buyer workflows differ. Enter one only after a paying customer confirms the same work-readiness need. BGC already sells corridor risk tools beyond pipelines, so cross-sector competition is real. [S8]

## 7. MVP: one connected story, not three dashboards

**Demo title:** *One kilometre. Three constraints. One decision.*

1. **Change detected:** The live or cached Edmonton development data shows a new permit near a sample corridor. Show its source, date and location. Do not assert it affects a verified gas transmission line unless validated.
2. **Hazard context:** Display the existing geohazard screening and its data quality. Explain that the hazard score is a priority indicator, **not** a failure probability.
3. **Proposed work:** A fictional inspection or repair request appears for that segment. A real technical professional would normally choose the appropriate action.
4. **Land check:** A clearly labelled **illustrative agreement** contains an access or notice requirement. The platform flags it and keeps unknown obligations as **unverified**.
5. **Human approval:** A named reviewer confirms a proposed action only after the relevant conditions are checked. n8n records a decision and produces a report or export.
6. **Closing frame:** The judge sees **before: scattered evidence and an incomplete review; after: a traceable preliminary work-readiness packet**. Do not claim to have measured time savings yet.

**Demo success condition:** A non-engineer can explain the problem and the proposed value in one sentence; a technical judge can see that source data, rules, approvals and unknowns are kept separate.

**Pitch opening:** “A pipeline crosses hundreds of properties and environments. Before one repair crew can start, teams may need to check changing development, ground conditions and agreements with landowners. TerraCorridor brings that information into one review—so teams know what needs attention before work begins.”

## 8. Validation plan: measure actual paid work

**Goal by November 27:** 10 conversations sought (five operators; five consultants), with at least two walkthroughs of real historical cases if possible. These are targets, not completed interviews. Prioritize role fit over hitting a count.

**Interview script — ask about the last real case, not the idea:**

1. What was the last corridor crossing, integrity dig, or maintenance job your team prepared?
2. Who received the request and which documents did they gather?
3. What systems already handled the review? What was still done manually?
4. What delayed approval or the start date? Did missing land or environmental information matter?
5. How many cases like this did you handle last month or year? How many people and hours were involved?
6. Does permit or subdivision information reach you before a formal request? When and from whom?
7. Would reviewing a **fictionalized or anonymized historical case** be permissible? Who would approve that?
8. Who owns the budget, and what evidence would justify a trial?
9. What would make our approach unacceptable or less useful than your current tool?
10. Would you consider a documented, non-binding pilot with no real operational decisions made by the prototype?

**Validation scoreboard:**

| Metric | How to measure | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| Frequency | Completed requests or work packets/year | Interview notes, anonymized counts |
| Preparation time | Minutes from intake to reviewer-ready packet | Timed baseline vs prototype |
| Quality | Missing requirements, false flags, evidence completeness | Review by a practitioner |
| Earlier warning | Date source appeared vs date current process flagged it | Dated historical source records |
| User trust | Would reviewer rely on it for preliminary screening? | Recorded objections and conditions |
| Commercial interest | Named sponsor, scope, pilot next step | Real written confirmation; no invented LOI |

**Stop conditions:** If two or more knowledgeable customers show that their existing system already does the full workflow well, narrow the proposition. If land obligations cannot be mapped to the correct segment, do not call the package “work-ready.” If the forecast performs no better than a simple baseline, avoid predictive superiority claims.

**Team conclusion:** Keep ROW as the company's purpose. Test operator and consultant demand on equal footing. Use development forecasting to identify early changes, geohazards to understand site conditions and land agreements to identify access constraints. Package these into **one review that a human can trust**. The next proof is not a bigger dashboard—it is a real user demonstrating that this review is needed, accurate and valuable.
